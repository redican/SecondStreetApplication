2nd Street through time

Jackson Ward, Richmond

prototype

From Reconstruction-era commerce to post-highway revival, Second Street in Jackson Ward has been a living document of Black life in Richmond. This map lets you walk through six eras of that history — seeing who lived here, what they built, and what was taken away.

Historical maps have been georeferenced by students at the University of Richmond. Parcels, points of interest, and era overviews are drawn from archival records, Sanborn fire insurance maps, and community documentation.

About this project

This is a collaborative digital humanities project developed with the Spatial Analysis Lab at the University of Richmond. Students georeference historical Sanborn maps and compile archival data on 2nd Street's built environment across time.

Some eras are still in production as students complete their georeferencing. Modern OpenStreetMap is shown as a reference layer for those eras.

Harlem of the South

The Hippodrome and other venues hosted Duke Ellington, Cab Calloway, and Bill "Bojangles" Robinson. Despite Jim Crow, 2nd Street thrived as a center of Black art, business, and nightlife.
